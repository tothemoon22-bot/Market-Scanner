# Cost Model

**Status:** Phase 0 deliverable. Every detector threshold derives from this file.

Generated by `python -m src.research.cost_model` from `src/venues/kalshi/fees.py`.
Do not hand-edit the tables — change the fee model and re-run. The evidence
behind the coefficients is in [`venues/kalshi/fees.md`](venues/kalshi/fees.md).

## The formula

```
fees = round up(0.07 x multiplier x C x P x (1-P))     # taker
fees = round up(0.0175 x multiplier x C x P x (1-P))   # maker, where charged
```

`P` in dollars, `C` in contracts, ceiling to the next whole cent. There is no
settlement fee, so a position held to expiry pays on entry only.

Three structural properties matter more than the coefficient:

1. **The ceiling applies to the whole fill, not per contract.** A 1-lot at 50c
   pays 2c, a 14% surcharge over the 1.75c arithmetic rate.
2. **Fees are assessed per fill.** An order that fills in four pieces rounds up
   four times. With `max_order_contracts = 10`, partial fills are likely and
   this is not a rounding footnote.
3. **A basket pays the ceiling once per leg.** An N-leg basket carries up to
   N x $0.0099 of pure rounding loss on top of the arithmetic fee.

## Headline: 100 contracts

Round trip = cross the spread in, cross the spread out at the same price. For a
no-arbitrage basket held to settlement, the relevant column is **Entry fee**.

| Price | Entry fee | Round trip | Entry c/contract | Round trip c/contract | Round trip % of stake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 25c | $1.32 | $2.64 | 1.3200c | 2.6400c | 10.56% |
| 50c | $1.75 | $3.50 | 1.7500c | 3.5000c | 7.00% |
| 75c | $1.32 | $2.64 | 1.3200c | 2.6400c | 3.52% |

The percentage column is a misleading way to look at this and is included only
because it is the conventional framing. The number that binds is cents per
contract, because the payoff is fixed at $1 and edges are quoted in cents.

These match the published fee table exactly ($1.32 / $1.75 / $1.32), which is
the acceptance test in `tests/test_fees.py`.

## Per-contract cost by order size (taker)

The size we can actually trade under the Phase 4 risk defaults is 10 contracts.

| Contracts | 25c | 50c | 75c |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2.0000c | 2.0000c | 2.0000c |
| 10 | 1.4000c | 1.8000c | 1.4000c |
| 100 | 1.3200c | 1.7500c | 1.3200c |
| 1000 | 1.3130c | 1.7500c | 1.3130c |

At our size the effective rate is 1.4c–1.8c per contract per leg. Detectors must
compute the fee at the size actually available, never at the asymptotic rate.

## Detector 1 (complementary pair) break-even

Buy YES at `a` and NO at `1-a`, both crossing the spread, hold to settlement.

| YES ask | NO ask | Fee (100 lots) | Fee c/contract | Max payable sum | Ticks of edge needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5c | 95c | $0.68 | 0.68c | 99.32c | 1 |
| 10c | 90c | $1.26 | 1.26c | 98.74c | 2 |
| 25c | 75c | $2.64 | 2.64c | 97.36c | 3 |
| 40c | 60c | $3.36 | 3.36c | 96.64c | 4 |
| 50c | 50c | $3.50 | 3.50c | 96.50c | 4 |

The tick is 1c, so "ticks of edge needed" is the number of whole cents of
mispricing that must be *visible on the book* before the trade is worth doing.
Near the middle of the range that is four ticks. **The fee gate is strictly
easier to clear at extreme prices** — 1 tick at 5c/95c against 4 ticks at
50c/50c. If a complementary edge exists anywhere, it is in the tails.

## Detector 2 (event basket) break-even

Uniform basket, one leg per outcome, 100 lots each, all legs crossing.

| Legs | Price per leg | Fee (100 lots) | Fee c/contract | Max payable sum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 50c | $3.50 | 3.50c | 96.50c |
| 3 | 33c | $4.65 | 4.65c | 95.35c |
| 4 | 25c | $5.28 | 5.28c | 94.72c |
| 5 | 20c | $5.60 | 5.60c | 94.40c |
| 10 | 10c | $6.30 | 6.30c | 93.70c |
| 20 | 5c | $6.80 | 6.80c | 93.20c |

A 10-outcome basket must be buyable for 93.7c or less. The per-leg fee falls as
legs get cheaper, but the count grows faster, so the gate tightens
monotonically with leg count. Wide baskets are the *least* promising place to
look, which is the opposite of the intuition that more legs means more chances
for one to be mispriced.

## Maker vs taker

| Price | Taker (100 lots) | Maker, quadratic | Maker, with_maker_fees |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 25c | $1.32 | $0.00 | $0.33 |
| 50c | $1.75 | $0.00 | $0.44 |
| 75c | $1.32 | $0.00 | $0.33 |

**Resting orders are free on 98.8% of series** (10,613 of 10,755 surveyed carry
`fee_type: quadratic`, where maker fees do not exist). That is the single
largest cost lever available and it is unusable for this strategy: a resting
order is not a fill, and a basket that fills on three legs out of four is an
uncovered directional bet, which Phase 4 explicitly forbids. Any future
"post the basket passively" idea has to answer the leg-risk question first.

## Open questions for Phase 1/2 measurement

- **Per-fill rounding in practice.** How often does a 10-lot fill in more than
  one piece? Each extra piece costs up to 0.99c. Measurable from the fills
  ledger in Phase 4; approximable in Phase 2 from trade prints.
- **`flat` fee type.** No live series uses it and we have not confirmed its 2026
  coefficient. `FeeModel.is_verified` is False for it and detectors must skip
  those series rather than guess.
- **Fee changes mid-scan.** `/series/fee_changes` and `/events/fee_changes`
  publish scheduled overrides. Both are empty today. Poll them; a series that
  changes fee type invalidates cached thresholds.
